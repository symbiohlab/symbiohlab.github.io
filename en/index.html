Laboratory of Microbial Symbiosis · Changwon National University

How do different organisms
come to live together?

We study how associations between organisms and their microbes work, how they shift with environment, and how they arose over evolutionary time.

ResearchJoin us →

In nature, organisms rarely live alone. Mycorrhizal fungi and a wide range of microbes inhabit plant roots. In lichens, fungi and photosynthetic partners form an association so close that the two are seen as a single organism. Arthropods and aquatic environments likewise harbour microbial communities that shape their hosts and are shaped by them in turn.

We use the term symbiosis in its broad sense. It covers not only mutually beneficial associations but also commensal and antagonistic ones — the full range of ways in which different organisms live together.

We work across lichens, plants, arthropods and aquatic systems, because comparing distantly related hosts is what allows us to tell host-specific phenomena apart from principles that recur across symbioses.

QUESTIONS

The questions we ask

What lives together?

Diversity and taxonomy of symbiotic organisms

We collect in the field, isolate and culture microbes, and describe what we find using morphological and molecular phylogenetic evidence. Sequencing genomes and examining how far sequence data can be trusted are part of the same work.

How, and under what conditions, do they live together?

Function and ecological context of symbiotic interactions

We examine what hosts and microbes do to one another, and how those relationships change with climate, season and habitat. Living together does not always mean the same thing, and it does not stay the same over time.

How did these associations arise?

Origin and evolution of symbiosis

We trace how symbiotic organisms diverged and spread, using phylogenetics, population genetics and genomic data.
